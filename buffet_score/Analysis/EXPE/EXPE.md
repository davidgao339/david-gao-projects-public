# Expedia Group (EXPE) - Fundamental Analysis

Here is an analysis of **Expedia Group (NASDAQ: EXPE)** applying the Buffett/Munger fundamental framework, grounded in their Q2 2026 earnings and financial data.

### Step 1: Character & Execution of Leadership (Earnings Call Evidence)

*   **CEO Profile & Track Record:** Ariane Gorin assumed the role of CEO in May 2024. Her background is rooted in B2B growth and enterprise sales, having spent a decade at Microsoft and subsequently serving as President of Expedia for Business, where she successfully drove a 33% increase in B2B revenue in 2023. Her major successes involve accelerating this highly profitable B2B segment and pushing a "traveler-centric" AI integration strategy. The primary challenges (or historical "failures" of the firm she inherited) include managing the transition away from a historically siloed brand structure and high dependency on raw Google SEO traffic, pivoting the company to defend against new generative AI aggregation threats.
*   **Capital Allocation:** Management demonstrates a disciplined, shareholder-friendly approach to the \$1.28 billion in Free Cash Flow generated in Q2 2026. Rather than pursuing "empire-building" M&A at any cost, they are aggressively shrinking the float: repurchasing \$200 million in stock (approx. 880,000 shares) in the quarter alone while maintaining a \$0.48 quarterly dividend. Organic CapEx is heavily focused on AI technology (e.g., the *Layla* conversational app integration) to improve structural efficiencies. 
*   **Incentive Alignment:** In recent calls, leadership's tone has clearly shifted from chasing raw, unadjusted B2C revenue growth toward structurally profitable B2B volume and Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance based specifically on an expected 150 to 175 basis point expansion in Adjusted EBITDA margins, indicating an internal alignment with cash generation over vanity top-line metrics.
*   **Communication Style:** Management has been relatively transparent regarding margin pressures in their explosive B2B segment. During the Q2 2026 call, leadership clearly delineated that while B2B Adjusted EBITDA margin contracted slightly year-over-year (sitting at 24.8%) due to partner mix and M&A integration costs, the overall consolidated margin expanded by 196 basis points (to 25.9%). They did not obscure the B2B margin compression behind non-GAAP tricks, but rather explained the unit economic trade-off of acquiring lower-margin, high-volume partner traffic.

### Step 2: Business Breakdown & Unit Economics

*   **Revenue Mechanics:** Expedia operates a two-pronged hook. The B2C hook (Expedia, Vrbo, Hotels.com) requires heavy top-of-funnel performance marketing spend (Google Ads) to acquire retail customers. The B2B back-end monetization engine provides white-label API and template solutions to airlines, banks, and corporate travel portals, allowing Expedia to take a cut of the bookings generated by partners' existing traffic. 
*   **Segment Profitability:** The B2C segment relies on a high Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) but yields higher gross margins per booking. Conversely, the B2B segment operates on lower gross margins (due to revenue sharing with partners) but requires virtually zero direct performance marketing, resulting in a highly efficient, volume-driven profit center.
*   **Growth Vectors:** The B2B segment is the absolute growth vector, marking its **20th consecutive quarter of double-digit growth** (21% YoY gross booking growth to \$10.7B in Q2 2026). This expansion is driven by new enterprise customer acquisition and deeper integration (higher wallet share) with existing partners, rather than pure consumer NRR.
*   **Cash Flow Conversion:** As an Online Travel Agency (OTA), Expedia operates a beautiful "merchant model." Customers pay Expedia upfront for a hotel stay, but Expedia pays the hotel *after* the stay occurs. This generates massive deferred revenue float and negative working capital. Consequently, Operating Cash Flow frequently exceeds Net Income, leading to exceptional FCF conversion rates with minimal tangible CapEx required.

### Step 3: Economic Moat & Durability

*   **Switching Costs & Ecosystem Friction:** In the B2C segment, switching costs are notoriously low (a traveler can easily check Booking.com or Google Flights). However, in the rapidly accelerating B2B segment, switching costs are immense. Once a major bank or airline integrates Expedia’s API to power their loyalty travel portal, the technical and operational pain of ripping out that infrastructure to switch to a competitor is a massive deterrent.
*   **Pricing Power:** While Expedia cannot dictate hotel prices, its consolidated scale allows it to maintain steady "take rates" (commissions) from fragmented independent hotels that desperately need distribution.
*   **Cost & Scale Advantages:** This is the core of Expedia's moat. Building a two-sided global marketplace of millions of properties and millions of travelers requires billions of dollars and decades of time. This scale, combined with negative working capital dynamics, creates an asset-light structure that is nearly impossible for a new entrant to replicate organically.
*   **Intangible Assets / Brand:** Brand equity is strong (Vrbo for alternative accommodations, Hotels.com, Expedia), driving a baseline of direct, organic traffic that bypasses the Google search tax, structurally lowering blended CAC compared to smaller peers.

### Step 4: Industry Structure & Competitive Dynamics

*   **Market Structure:** The OTA market is a global duopoly dominated by Booking Holdings and Expedia Group, with Airbnb acting as an adjacent major player in alternative accommodations. 
*   **Competitive Threats:** 
    *   *Bottom (Aggregators):* Google is the ultimate apex predator, constantly threatening to move further down the booking funnel and capture more top-of-funnel economics. 
    *   *Top (Premium Brands):* Major hotel chains (Marriott, Hilton) aggressively push direct-booking campaigns and loyalty programs to bypass OTA commissions entirely.
*   **Disruption Vectors:** AI automation represents the largest secular shift. If conversational AI (like ChatGPT or specialized agents) can perfectly plan and book complex itineraries by scraping the web, it could bypass the traditional OTA "front door." Expedia is actively trying to neutralize this threat by embedding AI directly into its apps (e.g., the *Layla* acquisition), ensuring they own the AI-driven booking layer rather than being disrupted by it.

## Appendix 1: Business Specific Q&A

**Q1: What's the profit distribution like between B2B and B2C?**
**A1:** As of Q2 2026, the profitability dynamics present a dual-engine narrative. While Expedia does not strictly break out absolute GAAP net income per segment, we can look at their Adjusted EBITDA (which totaled **\$1.119 billion** for the quarter) and margins:
*   **B2C Margin Improvement:** The B2C segment has a higher absolute Adjusted EBITDA margin, expanding by 380 basis points to **33.2%**. This is largely due to disciplined, highly efficient direct marketing spend (direct marketing costs fell to 4.7% of gross bookings). While top-line growth is slower (8%), it serves as the highly cash-generative engine, producing the lion's share of the firm's absolute profit.
*   **B2B Margin Pressures:** The B2B segment is the primary top-line growth engine (21% booking growth, 23% revenue growth) but operates at a slightly lower and compressed margin profile. B2B Adjusted EBITDA margin sat at **24.8%** (a contraction of 258 bps YoY) due to less favorable partner mix and the integration costs of recent acquisitions. 

Overall, B2B drives the volume and structural growth (\$10.7B in gross bookings), while B2C currently captures higher margin efficiency. Combined, they resulted in a blended consolidated Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion of 196 basis points to 25.9% (yielding \$1.119 billion in Adjusted EBITDA and \$878 million in total Net Income).

**Q2: What are the headwinds that erode top-line growth or margin growth?**
**A2:** There are distinct, structural headwinds impacting both the top and bottom lines:
*   **Top-Line Headwinds (Revenue/Bookings):** 
    *   **Aviation Weakness:** Expedia reported a 13% decline in airline-related revenue and a 5% drop in booked air tickets in Q2 2026. Flights act as a top-of-funnel acquisition channel, so weakness here hurts broader volume.
    *   **Regional Demand & FX:** Macroeconomic sluggishness in outbound European travel, combined with continuous Foreign Exchange (FX) headwinds, acts as a drag on global reported revenue growth.
*   **Margin Headwinds (Profitability):** 
    *   **B2B Partner Mix:** The primary driver of margin compression (a 258 bps drag in the B2B segment) is an unfavorable partner mix. As Expedia signs larger enterprise partners, those partners command a larger revenue share, compressing the take-rate and margin.
    *   **M&A & AI Investment Costs:** Aggressive integration costs from acquisitions (like CarTrawler) and heavy capital expenditures in AI (like Layla) drag down near-term operating margins.
    *   **Paid Traffic Dependence:** In the B2C segment, a structural, long-term margin headwind is the ever-increasing cost of performance marketing (Google Ads), capping how much efficiency they can wring out of direct marketing before growth stalls.

**Q3: What is Layla? What is the objective of acquiring it? How much cash are they burning?**
**A3:** 
*   **What it is:** Layla is an AI-native conversational trip planning and booking platform that Expedia Group acquired in July 2026. It uses generative AI to help travelers discover destinations, build itineraries, and book flights and hotels through a natural language chat interface.
*   **The Objective:** Expedia's primary objective with Layla is to aggressively bridge the gap between "inspiration" and "transaction." By owning the AI conversational interface, Expedia ensures it doesn't become a commoditized "dumb pipe" supplier to third-party AI assistants (like ChatGPT or Google). It allows Expedia to own the end-to-end user journey—combining Layla's highly engaging frontend personalization with Expedia's massive backend supply and pricing data.
*   **Cash Burn:** The specific financial terms of the Layla acquisition and its standalone "cash burn" were not publicly disclosed. However, Expedia Group itself is highly profitable and cash-generative (producing \$1.28 billion in Free Cash Flow in Q2 2026). Rather than viewing Layla through the lens of a burning startup, Expedia treats it as a strategic CapEx/R&D investment funded easily by the firm's core cash flows to defend its moat against secular AI disruption.

**Q4: The industry is a duopoly. What's the pricing power like? Is this a situation of cereals or airlines?**
**A4:** The OTA duopoly (Booking Holdings and Expedia Group) possesses pricing power that aligns much closer to the **cereal** industry than the airline industry, though with a digital twist.
*   **Not like Airlines:** The airline industry is fiercely competitive on price, incredibly capital-intensive, and suffers from structurally low margins and devastating price wars (a "race to the bottom"). OTAs, by contrast, are asset-light software platforms with sky-high gross margins. They do not engage in mutually destructive price wars because their primary competition is based on marketing spend and loyalty ecosystems.
*   **Like Cereals (Monopsonistic Power):** Similar to how Kellogg's and General Mills dictate terms to grocery stores for premium shelf space, OTAs wield immense pricing power over their *suppliers* (independent hotels). Because they control the digital "shelf space" (the front-page search results), they can charge high "take rates" (commissions of 15% to 25%+) to fragmented independent hotels that desperately need visibility.
*   **The Caveat (Lack of Consumer Pricing Power):** While they extract heavy rents from hotels, OTAs do *not* have monopolistic pricing power over the end *consumer*. Consumers face zero switching costs and can easily cross-shop between Expedia, Booking, and Google. Therefore, their pricing power is strictly **monopsonistic** (power over the supplier) rather than monopolistic (power over the buyer).

## Appendix 2: Munger Mental Models (Q&A)

**Q1 (Invert Practice): What must Expedia *not* do to avoid failure?**
Inverting the problem, Expedia must avoid four catastrophic errors to prevent structural decline:
1. **Becoming a Pure "Google Arbitrageur":** Relying entirely on Google Ads rather than driving direct app traffic (OneKey) allows Google to extract 100% of their margin.
2. **Missing the AI Interface:** If travelers shift to conversational AI (like ChatGPT), Expedia must own that interface. Becoming a "dumb pipe" backend supplier to third-party AI agents would obliterate their gross margins.
3. **Alienating B2B Enterprise Partners:** B2B is their growth engine. If their API becomes unreliable or they demand excessive revenue splits, major banks and airlines will switch competitors.
4. **Squeezing Hotel Suppliers Past the Breaking Point:** Expedia's monopsonistic pricing power (similar to cereal giants dictating shelf space) relies on hotel fragmentation. Pushing take-rates too high accelerates hotel consolidation (e.g., Marriott/Hilton), giving suppliers the scale to ruthlessly negotiate commissions down.

**Q2 (Lollapalooza Effect): Are there converging trends creating an extreme outcome?**
*   **The Positive Lollapalooza (Embedded Travel):** The convergence of global experiential travel demand with the rise of "embedded finance" (every bank, credit card, and loyalty program wanting a travel portal). Expedia's B2B white-label tech is perfectly positioned to capture this converging wave, driving massive, frictionless volume.
*   **The Negative Lollapalooza (The Great Squeeze):** The convergence of generative AI (threatening top-of-funnel customer acquisition) with massive hospitality consolidation (threatening bottom-of-funnel supply). If AI agents monopolize the consumer relationship while mega-hotel chains monopolize the room supply, Expedia’s position as the middleman could be severely crushed.

**Q3 (Moats and "Surfing"): Is Expedia fighting a headwind or riding a massive wave?**
Expedia is currently surfing two massive waves, but facing one secular headwind:
*   **Riding the Wave:** The B2B segment is effortlessly surfing the wave of enterprise loyalty programs (20+ quarters of double-digit growth). They are providing the essential "pick and shovel" API infrastructure for global travel rewards.
*   **Fighting the Headwind (AI Disruption):** The shift from traditional search engines to conversational AI is a massive headwind. To defend its moat and avoid disruption, Expedia recently acquired **Layla** (an AI-native trip planner). Rather than fighting the AI wave, Expedia's objective is to own the "inspiration to transaction" interface, combining Layla's highly engaging frontend with Expedia's massive backend supply data.

**Q4 (Second-Order Thinking): What are the second or third-order consequences of the current strategy?**
*   **B2C Commoditization via B2B Success:** The market cheers Expedia's 21% YoY booking growth in B2B. However, the *second-order consequence* of powering travel portals for major banks (like Chase or Amex) is that Expedia is actively training consumers to book travel through their credit card apps rather than Expedia.com. Over the long term, they risk commoditizing their highly profitable B2C brand.
*   **Structural Margin Compression:** Because B2C operates at a higher margin (33.2% Adjusted EBITDA) than B2B (24.8%), the second-order effect of B2B structurally outgrowing B2C is perpetual, blended margin compression. As B2B becomes a larger share of the total pie, Expedia will have to rely purely on absolute dollar volume scale rather than percentage margin expansion.
