# Expedia Group (EXPE) - Investment Strategy & Exit Plan

## The Thesis
The core investment thesis for Expedia is predicated entirely on the highly profitable, structural expansion of their B2B segment (white-label API and template solutions for banks, airlines, and corporate portals). The legacy B2C/DTC business (Expedia, Vrbo, Hotels.com) is viewed as a mature, stale cash cow that is facing increasing top-of-funnel acquisition costs from Google. The objective is to ride the B2B volume growth while treating the B2C segment merely as a funding source for capital returns.

## Earnings Call Checklist (The "Exit Triggers")
Monitor the following metrics relentlessly in each quarterly report. If any of these thesis-breakers occur, it signals an immediate exit strategy:

### 1. Deceleration of B2B Gross Bookings Growth
B2B has posted over 20 consecutive quarters of double-digit growth (e.g., 21% YoY in Q2 2026). 
*   **Exit Trigger:** If B2B bookings growth decelerates to single digits, the core growth engine has stalled.

### 2. Severe Unjustified Margin Collapse in B2B
Expedia trades margin for volume in B2B (e.g., margins compressed to 24.8% in Q2 2026 to secure enterprise partners). This is acceptable *only* if volume is exploding.
*   **Exit Trigger:** If B2B Adjusted EBITDA margin drops significantly (e.g., approaching 20%) *without* a corresponding massive spike in new enterprise deal volume, unit economics are deteriorating and they are losing negotiating leverage with partners.

### 3. Loss of Marquee Enterprise Partners (Churn)
The B2B moat relies on heavy technical integration (ecosystem lock-in).
*   **Exit Trigger:** If management notes on a call that a major partner (a large credit card company or airline) is leaving to build their platform in-house or defecting to competitors like Booking.com/Hopper.

### 4. Capital Misallocation (The B2C Panic Pivot)
The thesis requires management to accept B2C as a slow-growth cash cow and use the FCF to shrink the float (share repurchases) and invest in B2B/AI tech.
*   **Exit Trigger:** If management suddenly halts buybacks and dumps billions of dollars into a massive top-of-funnel consumer marketing campaign (Google Ads, TV spots) to desperately revive the B2C brand. This indicates panic, a misunderstanding of their structural disadvantages vs Google, and active destruction of capital.
